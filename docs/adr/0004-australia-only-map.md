# ADR 0004: An Australia-only map, built around the Nearly Broadband Network

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-01
- Decided by: Christopher Hawker

## Context

This repository is a fork of Series of Tubes, a world-scale game about building an internet backbone. The fork
becomes an Australian game: the player runs an ISP and hosting provider. In Australia, retail broadband runs
over one national wholesale network (NBN Co, in the game the Nearly Broadband Network). A retail provider can
only sell in an area once it reaches that area's Point of Interconnect (one of 121 POIs), buys an NNI port
there, and hauls the traffic back to its own network over a leased wave or dark fibre. Transit, peering,
dedicated servers and colocation round out the business.

The world map cannot carry that: Australia filled about 230 × 220 px of it, with 8 cities, and the
progression ran on continents and a global Tier 1 club.

## Decision

- **The map is Australia**, bounded (it no longer wraps), with every NBN POI as a node, the capital-city data
  center hubs, cable landing stations, and a few offshore gateways (Singapore, Jakarta, Port Moresby, Guam,
  Nouméa, Auckland, Suva, Honolulu, Los Angeles) reached by subsea cable. Gateways that do not fit the frame
  are drawn as edge stubs; their distances stay real.
- **Regions are states and territories** (`nsw vic qld wa sa tas nt act`, plus `int` for gateways). Every rule
  that counted continents counts states.
- **Generated data.** The coastline and the POI list are generated by `scripts/build-au-map.js` from committed
  inputs in `scripts/data/` (Natural Earth, public domain; NBN Co's published POI list), so they can be
  rebuilt and checked (`--check`).
- **NBN as of 2026:** to sell broadband behind a POI you need a POP there, an NNI port, and backhaul to your
  network; you pay NBN an AVC charge per subscriber. There is no CVC (it was phased out after the 2023 Special
  Access Undertaking variation).
- **Hosting is a business of its own:** colocation racks and dedicated servers in data center space you lease
  or build.
- **Old world saves are retired, not converted.** Their cities, links and networks no longer exist. A save
  without `mapV` is never run through `sanitize()` (which would strip it and let the next autosave write the
  stripped game back); it stays listed, can be exported as it is, and opening it explains the change and offers
  a fresh start.
- **One switch.** The rebuild happens on the `australia` branch and replaces `main` in one merge.

## Consequences

- World-only systems go: China, Russia and Korea routing variants, Gulf licences, DE-KICKS, Pitcairn and the
  rest, with their state, strings and tests.
- Puzzles, Architect runs, scenarios and the weekly generators stay, rebuilt on the Australian map (removed
  later: see the amendment of 2026-11-04).
- Performance cannot be compared with the old `main` across the switch (the fixture's cities no longer exist).
  The first commit with the Australian fixture is tagged `au-perf-base`, and later changes compare against it.
- GitHub Pages hosting (ADR 0001) and one assembled page (ADR 0002) are unchanged.

## Amendment (2026-11-01): the world comes back, Australia stays the game

Decided by Christopher Hawker, the same day, before the switch to `main`.

- **The map is the whole world again, and it wraps**, drawn at the scale Australia had alone (8471 map px round
  the world, the seam in the mid-Atlantic). Natural Earth 1:10m around Australia, the world map's 1:50m elsewhere,
  merged by `scripts/build-au-map.js`.
- **Overseas, only the PoPs that improve Australian traffic:** Singapore, Hong Kong, Tokyo, Auckland, Los Angeles
  and San Jose. Jakarta, Port Moresby, Guam, Nouméa, Suva and Honolulu are no longer cities; the cables that land
  there pass them as waypoints between real PoPs.
- **Transit is bought in an Australian data centre and nowhere else.** Not overseas (you go there to peer: exchange
  ports, PNIs, caches; you sell no broadband there either, no business customer has an end there) and not at a POI. A network starts in a data centre: the eight capitals and the regional
  colocation cities (Newcastle, the Gold Coast, the Sunshine Coast, the Central Coast, Wollongong, Toowoomba,
  Townsville, Bundaberg, Ballarat, Geraldton, Tamworth, Dubbo, Coffs Harbour, Launceston). The regional ones have
  no homes of their own: those come in at the POIs.
- **A POI is a handover point:** no transit, exchange or fabric ports there. Transit is hauled out from a data
  centre, directly or through other POIs (a daisy chain).
- **One interconnection fabric, Megapork**, which also reaches the overseas hubs: backhaul from there costs more
  than a wave on a subsea cable. Consolation Connect is gone.

## Amendment (2026-11-03): landing stations, the long cables, and what a data centre sells

Decided by Christopher Hawker, before the switch to `main`.

- **Cables stop at their landing stations.** Fiji, Guam, Hawaii, Jakarta and Oman are cities again (`ls: true`):
  a cable that lands there is split there, so using it onward needs a PoP at the landing. Nothing else is sold at
  one (no transit, exchange, PNI, cache, space, homes or customers). This replaces the waypoints of the first
  amendment.
- **The far ends of the cables from Australia are PoPs:** Ashburn, London and Marseille join the peering hubs,
  reached by the Oh-Man Cable (Perth–Oman), See-Me-Wee 3 (Perth, Jakarta, Singapore, Oman, Marseille, London) and
  the Pond Hopper (London–Ashburn). Kuala Lumpur joins too (a real Next-Door-DC site), overland from Singapore.
  Your own cables stay inside the survey box round Australia, Asia and the Pacific.
- **Every Next-Door-DC and Equinox site is on the map by its real code** (`dcN` lists the codes); Port Hedland and
  Newman, real edge sites, become regional data centres.
- **Caches, PNIs and rack space are bought in data centres** (`inDc()`: an Australian data centre or an overseas
  hub; space only in Australia, where the customers are). A POI is a place to connect to from one. You start in a
  data centre.
- **The city lenses never fold:** their push is m·d/(1+d²/r²)^1.5, one-to-one for m under about 4.9, so a town on
  a lens rim (Traralgon) can no longer overtake the coast and land in the sea.

## Amendment (2026-11-04): one game, two doors

Decided by Christopher Hawker, before the switch to `main`.

- **Puzzles, Architect runs, scenarios and the three weekly challenges are removed**, with their generators
  (`scripts/weekly-*.js`), published files (`site/{puzzles,scenarios,architect}/weekly/`), the Sunday workflow and
  every rule only they used (scenario rules, Flood Season, the no-clock rule). The start screen has two doors,
  Normal and Sandbox.
- **Saves keep loading:** a scenario save plays on as a Normal game on its preset (`sanitize()` drops `scn` and its
  seed); a save claiming to be a puzzle loads as On-call, as before.
